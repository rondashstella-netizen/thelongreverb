Hidden Currents

Sound, Space, and the Architecture of Resonance

Ron Dash Stella · 2026

A musician-listener’s inquiry into what sound actually does: how rooms shape perception, how bodies receive vibration, how recording changes memory, and how acoustic knowledge survives through architecture, ritual, engineering, and practice.

The book moves from the ordinary act of listening into ancient acoustic spaces, modern signal systems, the Grateful Dead’s experiments in live sound, and the practical problem of building rooms that let sound remain dimensional. Hidden Currents stands on its own under The Long Reverb umbrella. It is unnumbered and is not a Bardos volume.

The Carriers

From the Wall of Sound to the King’s Chamber.

The central historical throughline follows an acoustic argument rather than fan mythology: Owsley Stanley’s demand for cleaner live sound, the Wall of Sound, Dan Healy’s continuation of that work, and the 1978 Egypt experiment in which the King’s Chamber itself became part of a live signal path.

A separate back-matter document, Declassified Listening Notes, contains the MKUltra / Project Pandora material as bounded historical context. It is an appendix to Hidden Currents, not another book and not the book’s governing frame.
